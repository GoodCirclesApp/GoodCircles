Passive & Recurring Funding

The lowest-labor money your nonprofit can raise.

A passive, recurring funding base is income that arrives whether or not you run a campaign — unrestricted, dependable, and almost free of staff time. It de-risks your organization, frees your team to do the mission, and gives funders the durability signal they reward. Here's how to build one.

Unrestricted & recurring
A passive funding base is recurring, unrestricted income your nonprofit receives with little to no ongoing effort — earned revenue, a monthly giving program, and everyday/passive giving where supporters fund you automatically as they live their lives. It's the most valuable money a nonprofit can raise because it's durable (it doesn't disappear when a campaign ends), flexible (you decide how to spend it), and nearly labor-free (it doesn't trade staff hours for dollars). Funders reward it because an organization with recurring income won't collapse without their grant.

Why a passive base beats one-time gifts

Most nonprofit fundraising trades labor for money: every event, appeal and grant cycle costs staff hours you can't get back. A passive base flips that equation. Once it's set up, it keeps producing — funding the gaps a campaign can't, covering the unrestricted costs grants won't, and giving you the financial stability that makes everything else easier. It's the difference between a paycheck and a tip jar.
